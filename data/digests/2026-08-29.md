## The week
Nvidia posted $96.2B in Q2 revenue, up 106% and its first triple-digit growth quarter in two years, then guided Q3 to $108B and FY28 to roughly $691B against a $570B consensus. The fund's read is that the market fixates on the wrong risk. Custom hyperscaler silicon gets the headlines, but a second buyer base is compounding faster: the ACIE segment (AI clouds, industrial, enterprise) grew 139% to $40.3B and 25% sequentially, versus hyperscalers at 101% and 13%. Sovereign AI and regional neoclouds tripled year over year. If Big Tech redirects spend to Trainium and TPUs, that cushion matters.

Pricing power shows up in the roadmap. Revenue per gigawatt climbs from Hopper's $18B to Grace Blackwell's $25B to Vera Rubin's $40B, and Vera Rubin delivers 10x more tokens per megawatt than GB200 on a CoreWeave benchmark. As the industry's KPI shifts from FLOPs to tokens per watt, that favors Nvidia. The caution sits on the balance sheet: $164.5B in contingent guarantees, $105B of it backing OpenAI's Ohio campus, so Nvidia is increasingly underwriting its own demand. The fund argues ACIE revenue may deserve a lower multiple for exactly that reason, and flags $236.55 as technical resistance, with January-quarter Vera Rubin volume the likely breakout catalyst.

Penguin Solutions got the harder look. FQ3 revenue hit a record $478.7M, up 47.6% and 39.6% sequentially, but the fund traces the surge to DRAM and NAND pricing, not the CXL KV-cache servers the bull case rests on. FQ4 guidance implies growth near-stalls at 5.5% QoQ, gross margin compressed to 28.1%, and operating cash flow swung negative to -$74.8M as inventory nearly doubled to $498.3M. The MemoryAI product claims 2x inference throughput and a 4-5x cost edge over HBM expansion, but it's a small slice today against Dell and Super Micro operating at 100-140x Penguin's AI server scale. A credible thesis, unproven execution.
