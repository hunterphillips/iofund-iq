## The week

Token demand is the spine of the week's research. Google's monthly token processing is up 330X in two years, and every major forecaster has undershot by 10-100X, with Dell's revised 2028 estimate already exceeded 2.4X. Reasoning and agentic workloads are the driver: multi-agent systems consume up to 15X more tokens than chatbots. The fund's read is that inference infrastructure is now the dominant AI hardware trade, and it split the beneficiaries across compute, networking, and power.

Storage is where that demand shows up in numbers. Seagate posted record margins on rising price per exabyte and falling cost per terabyte, with nearline supply allocated into 2028 and pricing power widening the gap between revenue and exabyte growth. Silicon Motion grew revenue 127% YoY on boot drives, though accelerating cash burn (three straight negative operating-cash quarters and a first-ever bank loan) is a real watch item. On the equipment side, Lam is riding a NAND conversion cycle now pulled forward before end of 2027, with the fund modeling far higher Lam share than consensus.

Power is the third leg. Bloom crossed $1B in quarterly revenue, up 166% YoY, with backlog growing faster still and the Brookfield financing line expanded 5x to $25B. Behind-the-meter power sidesteps ERCOT queue delays that can cost a 100 MW project ~$500M.

Meta was the exit. Revenue growth decelerated to 28%, margins fell 12 points, and free cash flow was nearly wiped out with capex at 97.5% of operating cash. The new compute-selling venture while leasing third-party capacity struck the fund as incoherent, so it closed the position after hours.
