## The week

Every major forecaster has been wrong on token growth by 10 to 100X, and the fund built the week's activity around that. Google's monthly token processing rose 330X in two years and 7X year-over-year; Dell's revised 2028 estimate of 57 quadrillion tokens is already being exceeded by roughly 2.4X in current run-rate. Reasoning and agentic workloads drive it: multi-agent systems burn up to 15X the tokens of a chatbot, coding agents 1,000X a code chat. The fund's read is that inference infrastructure, not training, now sets the pace of the hardware trade.

That thesis maps directly onto the buys. Compute, networking, and power are the three layers that benefit as cost per token falls but consumption rises faster. New positions in AAOI, LITE, and SITM sit on the optical and timing side; the SNDK add and the earlier storage work (Seagate, Silicon Motion) sit on the memory side. Seagate posted record margins with price per exabyte climbing while cost per TB falls on HAMR, and nearline supply is already allocated into 2028. Silicon Motion grew revenue 127% YoY on boot drives, though three straight quarters of negative operating cash flow and a first-ever bank loan are worth watching. The SMH hedge came off, consistent with adding risk into the trade.

Bloom is the power leg. Q2 crossed $1B in revenue, up 166% YoY, with backlog growing faster still and Brookfield's financing commitment expanding 5X to $25B. The fund's 2025 entry is up over 1,100%.

Meta was the exit. Revenue growth decelerated to 28%, operating margin fell 12 points, capex consumed 97.5% of operating cash flow, and free cash flow dropped 91% to $784M. Management's plan to sell excess compute while leasing third-party capacity didn't reconcile. The fund closed the position and stated it prefers the companies collecting the capex, not spending it.
