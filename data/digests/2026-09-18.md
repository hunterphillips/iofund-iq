## The week

Micron anchored the week's memory argument. This cycle is being built against contracted demand rather than speculation, with SK Hynix seeing demand above capacity past 2030 and constraints holding through at least 2028. Micron's five-year customer agreements lock roughly 40% of revenue at Q2 2026 prices, which turns the story into one about margins and durability rather than headline growth. The numbers behind it are large: HBM went from about $4B in 2023 to $34.6B in 2025 and is modeled at $100B by 2027, driven partly by context windows expanding 230x in three years. Q2 2026 DRAM contract prices rose 58-63% sequentially. The fund's key point is that the uncapped 60% of revenue rolls onto next-gen HBM, DDR6, and LPDDR6 at higher prices, and consensus post-FY29 estimates (growth flat then sharply negative) undervalue that.

Dell's FQ2 supplied the demand-side confirmation. AI servers now run just over a third of ISG, while traditional servers grew 122% and storage became the margin lever that offsets AI-server gross-margin drag. FY27 AI server guidance sits at $74B, raised $24B from the original $50B, with backlog at 1.3x the full-year guide. Adjusted EPS of $7.04 beat by 43%, and operating margin hit 11.5%, the best in over a decade. Agentic AI is pulling CPU, server, and storage spend up alongside GPUs rather than instead of them.

The broadening thesis tied both together. Nvidia's enterprise and neocloud segment grew 138.5% against the hyperscalers' 101.2%, and Dell counts 6,500 enterprise/neocloud customers, most added in the last three quarters. Rack economics do the rest: Rubin racks carry roughly 2x Blackwell ASPs, and liquid cooling crosses 50% penetration in 2026 for the first time. That favors OEMs like Dell and Super Micro that ship ready-to-deploy racks. The specific Dell-versus-SMCI-versus-HPE stock call stays behind the paywall.
